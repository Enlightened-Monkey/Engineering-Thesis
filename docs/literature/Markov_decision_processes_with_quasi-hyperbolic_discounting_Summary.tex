\section*{Summary: Markov Decision Processes with Quasi-Hyperbolic Discounting}

\paragraph{Context and Motivation}
Exponential discounting yields time-consistent planning and Bellman's principle. Empirical present-bias motivates quasi-hyperbolic (QH) $(\beta,\delta)$ preferences.

\paragraph{Preference Model}
For reward stream $(r_t)_{t\ge0}$, at time $t$ the evaluation is
\[
  V_t = r_t + \beta \sum_{k\ge1} \delta^k r_{t+k},\quad \delta\in(0,1),\; \beta\in(0,1].
\]
Present-bias arises for $\beta<1$; $\beta=1$ recovers exponential discounting.

\paragraph{Time Inconsistency and Agent Types}
Time inconsistency: each ``self'' re-optimizes at their now. \emph{Naive} agents ignore this; \emph{sophisticated} agents anticipate and choose subgame-perfect equilibria.

\paragraph{MDP Formulation}
Finite MDP $(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{A},P,r)$ with bounded rewards. Bellman optimality fails globally; equilibrium analysis restores tractability.

\paragraph{Equilibrium and Existence}
In finite, time-homogeneous settings, stationary Markov equilibria exist under mild conditions. Randomization is generally unnecessary for generic rewards.

\paragraph{Computation}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Finite horizon: backward induction on the intrapersonal game.
  \item Infinite horizon: fixed point of a best-response operator; or coupled recursions separating immediate and continuation values.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Comparison with Exponential Discounting}
Bellman principle breaks; dynamic programming is recovered via equilibrium (sophisticated) or augmented formulations. Contraction/Blackwell arguments need adaptation.

\paragraph{RL Implications}
Separate immediate and continuation components: learn $\delta$-discounted continuation values and combine via $\beta$ for strictly future rewards. Modify TD/Q-learning targets accordingly.

\paragraph{Limitations}
QH is stylized; continuous/unbounded settings need additional conditions; partial sophistication yields different outcomes.

\paragraph{Key Takeaways}
QH induces time inconsistency, but stationary Markov equilibria often exist. Computation reduces to fixed points among successive selves; RL adapts by two-component targets.
